Project Roadmap & Gates

Trooth grows through strict, transparent phase gates tied to cost constraints and verified user demand (CLAUDE.md section 9).

Every gate has three parts and all three have to clear. Technical is a test that passes or fails. Commercial is a number. Desirability is a question with a yes or no answer, asked of somebody outside this project, and it is the only one of the three that cannot be satisfied by working harder.

Phase 0: The Benchmark

In progress
Timeline: Weeks 1-3 | Cost Ceiling: < $5/month (Actual: $0.00)

Ships the routing catalog of 5 verified sources, the connector registry, the snapshot and diff jobs, this site and the free Console, and Modafied's emblems rendered as they come.

Gate to proceed.
Technical: the archive persists across two consecutive cron runs, 5 sources are merged, and Modafied is grading at least 3 with its manifest pinned and verifying.
Commercial: spend stays under $5 a month.
Desirability: the fresh-agent test passes, and one outside reader asks for a source to be added.
Not satisfied yet: the snapshot cron writes to a runner disk it then discards, so no archive is accumulating.

Phase 1: The Free Pipe

Active / Next Up
Timeline: Weeks 4-8 | Cost Ceiling: < $20/month

Ships the single data connector: TypeScript Cloudflare Worker REST API and thin MCP server implementing the exact six tools. Every fetch returns an Ed25519 signed envelope with provenance.

Gate to proceed.
Technical: p95 under 400ms on the quickstart path, and every response carries an envelope with its rejected candidates listed.
Commercial: 25 distinct weekly active callers, at least one an autonomous production tool.
Desirability: one external developer replaces a direct API call with Trooth, and says why.

Phase 2: Paid Tier & Immutability

Gated
Timeline: Weeks 9-14 | Cost Ceiling: < $50/month

Ships Stripe metered billing, Pro tier ($39/mo for 100k requests), 1-year historical snapshot replay, schema change webhooks, and append-only Merkle transparency log anchored into Bitcoin via OpenTimestamps.

Gate to proceed.
Technical: the same query replayed with as_of returns byte-identical data and a byte-identical envelope hash.
Commercial: a first paying customer. No new cost-bearing feature is built until this is met.
Desirability: that customer renews or expands.

Phase 3: The Provider Marketplace

Future
Timeline: After Phase 2 Gate | Cost Model: Scales with revenue

Allows external data owners to list feeds, filters, or analysis layers. Trooth meters calls and handles automated payouts with a platform take rate. Grading stays with Modafied, listed feeds included.

Gate to proceed.
Technical: a provider's feed gets a Modafied scorecard without hand-holding.
Commercial: 3 providers listed and 1 paid feed actively transacting.
Desirability: a provider cites their scorecard publicly, without being asked.

Phase 4: Specialized Verticals & Licensing

Future
Timeline: After Phase 3 Gate

Regulatory data slices (openFDA MAUDE medical device reports), sports feeds graded against settled game outcomes, and licensed historical revision datasets.

Gate to proceed.
Technical: the vertical ships on the same envelope with no special case in the pipe.
Commercial: decided on revenue.
Desirability: a vertical user asks for a second feed.

Where Trooth sits

Trooth is one layer of three, and the split is deliberate. Trooth fetches external data and wraps it in a provenance envelope. KouroB answers repeated questions above it, by replaying short graded chains of small models rather than calling a frontier model every time. Modafied grades what both of them do, against named anchors, with open code.

Two of those layers route, and they route differently. Trooth decides which source answers a fetch, which is a lookup against a catalog. KouroB decides which models answer a query, which is a planning problem. Keeping them apart is why neither one can quietly become the other, and it is why the planner's failure modes are not the pipe's.

Kill / Pivot Rule (CLAUDE.md section 9)

If Phase 2 ends with zero paying customers and fewer than 50 weekly active keys, Trooth freezes at Phase 1, keeps the public benchmark running for brand reputation at under $10/month, and documents the retrospective in public experiments. We do not burn capital without proven market pull.
